### Theory

#### Definition of Drone Technology
Drone technology refers to the design, development, production, and application of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), commonly known as drones. These aircraft can operate without a human pilot onboard and are controlled remotely by a human operator or autonomously by onboard computers.
#### Components
- **X Frame**:
  - The X frame, as the name suggests, is the frame where pretty much all the components are anchored or attached. It provides structural support and determines the overall shape and size of the drone.

#### Different Types of Drones

- **Quadcopter**: A drone with four rotors, known for its stability and ease of control. It is commonly used for photography, videography, and recreational flying.
- **Hexacopter**: A drone with six rotors, providing more lift and redundancy in case of motor failure. It is often used for professional aerial photography and cinematography.
- **Octocopter**: A drone with eight rotors, offering even greater lift and stability. It is typically used for heavy-duty applications such as industrial inspections and search and rescue operations.

#### Categorization of UAVs
- **Based on Size**: UAVs can be categorized as very small (handheld), small (up to 25 kg), medium (25-150 kg), and large (over 150 kg).
- **Based on Range**: UAVs can be categorized as very short range (up to 5 km), short range (5-50 km), medium range (50-200 km), and long range (over 200 km).
- **Based on Wings**: UAVs can be categorized as fixed-wing, rotary-wing, and flapping-wing.
- **Based on Weight**: UAVs can be categorized as lightweight (under 2 kg), medium weight (2-25 kg), and heavyweight (over 25 kg).
