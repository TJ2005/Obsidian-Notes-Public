# Rules of Forensic Science
Crime is an unlawful act that is forbidden and punished by the State or the law. In other words, anything which is injurious to public welfare is a crime
### Rules of Forensic Science

- **Law 1: Individuality**
    - **"No two things in this universe are alike even when they are manufactured in the same machine one after the other"**. This means that **"Every object, natural or man-made, has an individuality which is not duplicated in any other object"**.
    - **Forensic Implications**: This law forms the fundamental basis of crime identification. Every item involved in a crime has its own individuality, and **establishing this uniqueness helps link the crime to the criminal**.

- **Law 2: Principle of Exchange (Locard's Principle)**
    - This principle was formulated by **Dr. Edmond Locard**, a French criminologist and pioneer in forensic science, known as the "Sherlock Holmes of France".
    - **Basic Principle**: It states: **"Every contact leaves a trace"** or, in simpler words, **"when two objects come into contact with each other a cross-transfer of material occurs"**.

- **Law 3: Law of Progressive Change**
    - **"Everything changes with the passage of time"**.
    - The **rate of change varies tremendously with different types of objects**, and this has a **giant impact on forensic science investigations**.
    - **Changes in Criminals**: Criminals undergo progressive changes over time, potentially becoming unrecognisable if not apprehended promptly, except perhaps through permanent characteristics like fingerprints or bone fractures, which may not always be available or dependable.
    - **Changes at the Scene of Crime**: A crime scene also undergoes rapid changes due to factors like weather, vegetable growth, and the presence of living beings (especially human beings). Longer delays in examination lead to greater changes, potentially making the scene unrecognisable.
    - **Changes in Evidences**: Evidence involved in a crime can be affected by external conditions and change gradually (e.g., firearm barrels loosen, metal objects rust, shoes acquire new wear patterns, tools gain new surface patterns). The degree of change depends on time elapsed, upkeep, and use/misuse of the object. Over time, an object might lose all practical identity in relation to a specific crime.
    - **Demand for Prompt Action**: This principle therefore **demands prompt action in criminal investigations** to ensure the integrity and relevance of evidence.

- **Law 4: Principle of Comparison**
    - This principle states: **"Only the likes can be compared"**.
    - **Example**: If a bullet found from a deceased's body is confirmed by expert opinion to have been fired from a high-velocity firearm, it would be useless to send a shotgun, pistol, or revolver for comparison with the firearm in question. This illustrates that **comparison is only meaningful between similar types of objects or traces**.

- **Law 5: Principle of Analysis**
    - **"The analysis can be no better than the sample analyzed"**.
    - This means that **if the sample quality is poor then so is the analysis done on the sample**.
    - **Example**: In a rape case, an investigating officer must properly dry and pack the victim's clothes carrying blood and semen stains to prevent affected areas from touching each other or the container walls. This ensures the integrity of the sample for forensic laboratory examination, such as determining the presence of semen and the blood group.

- **Law 6: Law of Probability**
    - All identifications, whether definite or indefinite, are made consciously or unconsciously **always on the basis of the law of probability**.
    - **Definition**: Probability refers to the likelihood of a specific event happening in a specific manner out of a number of ways in which the event has an opportunity to occur or not occur, compared to all possible outcomes.
    - **Examples**: If a forensic scientist receives a biological fluid as physical evidence, the chances of it belonging to a particular person will vary. Similarly, if a bank is robbed by a gang, investigators need to calculate the probability that the gang could be held responsible.

- **Law 7: Law of Circumstantial Facts**
    - This law relates to the **question of proof in a criminal trial**, which is becoming increasingly difficult due to its connection with people, law, and facts.
    - **Proving Facts**: Facts can be proved or disproved based on circumstances and physical evidence, as well as through witnesses.
    - **Witnesses**: Witnesses can be eyewitnesses or expert witnesses, with the latter basing their findings on scientific observations.
    - **Evaluation of Evidence**: The evidence is scrutinized by counsels and evaluated by courts.
    - **Reliability of Oral Evidence**: While dependence is placed on oral evidence from eyewitness observations, experiences and experiments have repeatedly shown that **oral evidence can sometimes be unreliable**. This highlights the importance of circumstantial facts and physical evidence in establishing proof.
