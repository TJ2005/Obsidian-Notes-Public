# Normal Distribution
A continuous Random Variable $X$ Is said to follow normal distribution with parameters
- $\mu$ ( Mean )
- $\sigma^2$ ( Variance^2 )
If its probability density function is given by

$$
f(x) = \frac{1}{\sigma \sqrt{ 2\pi }} e^{1/2(\frac{x-\mu}{\sigma})^2}
$$

For a normal Distribution 
- $mean$=$median$=$mode$=$\mu$ 
- The probability distribution curve is symmetric about the mean ($\mu$)
- 50% of the values are less than mean
- 50% of the values are greater than mean
- Coefficient of skewness is $0$
- $Z = \frac{x-\mu}{\sigma}$

## Standard Normal Variable
A standard normal variable is a normal variable with $\mu$ = 0 and Standard Deviation $\sigma$ = 1. Its Probability Density Function is given by $$\phi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 2\pi }}e^{z^2/2}$$
The standard normal variate is obtained from 
$$
Z=\frac{X-\mu}{\sigma}
$$
