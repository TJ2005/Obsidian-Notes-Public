# Multiplicative and Additive Forms of Complex Numbers




### **1. Additive Representation (Rectangular Form)**

This form expresses a complex number as the sum of its real and imaginary components:

$$z=x+iyz = x + iy$$

where

- $xx$ = real part,
- $yy$ = imaginary part,
- ii = imaginary unit $$(i2=−1i^2 = -1).$$

🔹 **Why is it called additive?**

- The real and imaginary parts are simply **added** together to represent the complex number.

---

### **2. Multiplicative Representation (Polar/Exponential Form)**

In polar or exponential form, the complex number is expressed as:

z=reiθz = r e^{i\theta}

or

z=r(cos⁡θ+isin⁡θ)z = r (\cos \theta + i \sin \theta)

where:

- r=∣z∣=x2+y2r = |z| = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2} (magnitude or modulus),
- θ=arg⁡(z)=tan⁡−1(yx)\theta = \arg(z) = \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{y}{x} \right) (argument or phase).

🔹 **Why is it called multiplicative?**

- The complex number is written as a product of its magnitude rr and a rotational factor eiθe^{i\theta} (Euler's formula), which encodes its direction in the complex plane.

---

### **Why Are They Not the Same?**

1. **Different Interpretations**:
    
    - The **additive** form directly represents a complex number using Cartesian coordinates.
    - The **multiplicative** form represents the same number in terms of **magnitude** and **direction** in the complex plane.
2. **Operations Behave Differently**:
    
    - Addition and subtraction are **simpler** in the additive form.
    - Multiplication and division are **easier** in the multiplicative form (since magnitudes multiply and angles add).
3. **Conceptual Difference**:
    
    - Additive form (x+iyx + iy) treats the complex number as a sum of two perpendicular components.
    - Multiplicative form (reiθr e^{i\theta}) treats it as a **scaled rotation** from the positive real axis.

---

### **Example: Compare the Forms**

Consider z=1+iz = 1 + i:

- **Additive Form:** z=1+iz = 1 + i
- **Multiplicative Form:** r=12+12=2r = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2} θ=tan⁡−1(11)=π4\theta = \tan^{-1} \left( \frac{1}{1} \right) = \frac{\pi}{4} z=2eiπ/4z = \sqrt{2} e^{i \pi/4}

Clearly, the two forms express the same number differently.

---

### **Conclusion**

The **additive form** is useful for direct computation and visualization in the Cartesian plane, while the **multiplicative form** is useful for analyzing complex numbers in terms of magnitude and rotation, particularly in multiplication, division, and root extraction.
